SLIIT RESEARCH · R26-ISE-002

Every rack. Every stage. Every harvest.

Grow. Sense. Protect.

An integrated, rack-level system for Sri Lankan oyster mushroom farms. It connects growth stage awareness, moisture stress modelling, CO₂ trend analysis and early disease detection in one mobile-assisted platform.

01 / THE IDEA

A smarter way to grow.

Oyster mushrooms are sensitive to the conditions around them, and in multi-rack farms every rack and shelf has its own micro-climate. Yet most decisions are still made by eye and by fixed schedule.

This research project from the Sri Lanka Institute of Information Technology (SLIIT) brings four capabilities into one platform: growth stage detection, moisture stress regulation, CO₂ stress prediction and disease detection with rack-level tracking, delivered through a mobile app and a portable sensing-and-actuation device.

Outputs are advisory. The farmer always makes the final decision on treatment and harvesting.

GROWTH STAGE

Growth Stage & Harvest Time

See how every rack is growing, and when it will be ready.

Explore component

Input

Rack images from the camera of the portable device.

Process

Image preprocessing for lighting and noise, then a CNN classifies early growth, pinning, fruiting and mature stages.

Output

Detected stage and estimated days to harvest, shown in the mobile app; stage context is passed to the moisture module.

Approach

CNN image classification with growing-degree-day based harvest estimation.

MOISTURE STRESS

Moisture Stress Regulation

Humidity read in the context of temperature and growth stage.

Explore component

Input

Rack-level temperature and relative humidity, plus detected growth stage.

Process

Vapour pressure deficit and a Transpiration Stress Index (TSI); rule-based decision logic.

Output

Moisture stress status, stage-aware recommendations and automatic mist sprayer / exhaust fan activation.

Approach

TSI modelling with rule-based control instead of fixed humidity thresholds.

CO₂ STRESS

CO₂ Stress Prediction

Catch invisible gas build-up before it harms the crop.

Explore component

Input

Continuous CO₂ readings from the rack-level sensor.

Process

Time-series trend analysis into a normalised CO₂ stress index; threshold and persistence checks.

Output

Early warnings, ventilation advice and, if stress persists, automatic image capture for validation.

Approach

Predictive trend analysis with hybrid image-based confirmation.

DISEASE DETECTION

Disease Detection & Rack Tracking

Spot green mold and black mold early, and know exactly where.

Explore component

Input

Smartphone images of mushroom bags with rack ID and bag ID.

Process

Validation, resizing and CLAHE, then YOLOv8 object detection of green mold (Trichoderma) and black mold (Aspergillus niger).

Output

Annotated result, history, report export and a colour-coded rack tracking grid; farmer confirms health status.

Approach

YOLOv8 detection, FastAPI inference service and MongoDB rack-level records.

Responsibility

M. I. Amana · IT22156624

03 / THE APPROACH

From signal to decision.

Sense → recognise → analyse → act → monitor.

  1. 1

    Sense

    Temperature, humidity and CO₂ readings and rack images are collected.

  2. 2

    Recognise

    Computer vision finds the growth stage and any contamination.

  3. 3

    Analyse

    TSI and CO₂ stress indices are computed in context.

  4. 4

    Act

    Mist sprayer or exhaust fan is triggered; warnings are sent.

  5. 5

    Monitor

    Rack history, alerts and reports track progress over time.

04 / THE RESEARCH DOMAIN

Practical on the farm. Rigorous in the lab.

Explore the literature, questions, objectives and methodology behind the project.

Literature survey

Studies of IoT and deep learning for mushroom farming show clear progress but leave gaps for rack-level, stage-aware management.

  • Environmental control. IoT systems monitor and automate temperature and humidity in oyster mushroom houses, including fuzzy-logic control, but mostly use fixed thresholds and treat temperature and humidity separately.
  • Humidity and transpiration. Botanical research shows humidity and temperature interact through vapour pressure deficit, so the same relative humidity can mean different stress at different temperatures.
  • Sri Lankan IoT study. An IoT system for local oyster mushroom farms combined monitoring, harvest detection, disease detection and yield prediction with CNN and LSTM models, but without rack-level contamination tracking.
  • Image-based disease detection. YOLOv5-based systems classify only healthy versus diseased; growing-bag detection with IoT and web apps shows the direction of travel.
  • Growth stage tracking. Vision work focuses on robotic harvesting rather than multi-stage classification and remaining-days estimates for growers.
Research gap
  • Moisture control relies on fixed relative-humidity thresholds, ignoring temperature interaction and growth stage.
  • CO₂ monitoring is disconnected from growth context and rarely validated by images.
  • Disease systems give broad labels and no rack or bag tracking.
  • Environmental control, disease detection and growth monitoring exist as separate silos, rarely tailored to Sri Lankan farms.
Research problem

How can farmers manage each rack according to its biological growth stage and true moisture stress, anticipate CO₂ stress and detect fungal disease early, when they currently depend on visual judgement, fixed misting schedules and manual inspection?

The core problem is the absence of an integrated, intelligent rack-level micro-climate and growth management system.

Research objectives

Main objective. Design and develop an intelligent, rack-level micro-climate and growth optimization system for oyster mushroom cultivation integrating growth stage detection, TSI-based moisture stress modelling, CO₂ stress prediction and image-based disease identification, to improve yield consistency and reduce crop loss.

  1. Accurately detect the biological growth stage at rack level using image analysis.
  2. Regulate moisture stress at rack level using temperature–humidity interaction and growth stage.
  3. Predict CO₂-induced physiological stress and validate prolonged stress with images.
  4. Detect and classify green mold and black mold using image-based techniques.
Methodology & evaluation

The project follows Design Science Research with an Agile development approach: identify the problem, specify requirements, collect and annotate data, build and train models, integrate, then evaluate.

  • Images are collected on farms with smartphones; temperature, humidity and CO₂ come from commercially available sensors at rack level.
  • Computer vision (CNN, YOLOv8) handles stage and disease detection; stress indices drive rule-based control.
  • Evaluation uses precision, recall, mAP and accuracy, response time, and user acceptance testing with growers.
Results, limitations & next steps

Early evaluation of the disease detection component reports precision 96.2%, recall 94.8% and mAP@50 95.5%; the growth stage classifier reports 92.4% accuracy. A four-week field trial with 15 growers on three farms rated the platform positively.

Limitations include dataset size and variety, lighting conditions, and dependence on connectivity. Future work: more pathogens and farms, edge inference for offline use, and Sinhala/Tamil interfaces.

System architecture

The platform connects the farmer’s mobile app, a backend with AI services and a shared database; the portable device senses and actuates at rack level.
